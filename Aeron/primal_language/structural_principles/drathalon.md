# Drathalon

- Rendered form: `Drathalon`
- Gloss: The Colossus-Father; Cataclysmic Beast progenitor of all humanoid-mass-scale kin
- Tier: structural principle

## Meaning

`Drathalon` names the [Vorothar](vorothar.md) who walks at mountain-scale — the titan-of-titans, whose footfall reshapes coastlines, whose stride spans valleys, whose silhouette can be mistaken on the horizon for a peak that has begun to move.

## Scope

`Drathalon` carries:

- Cataclysmic mass; humanoid scale raised past plausibility
- Progenitor-status for titans, jotnar, ettins, balrogs, oni, formorians, walking-mountains
- Foundational-strength as cosmic principle — `Drathalon` is what mortals mean when they speak of a being whose size is itself the answer
- One of the fourteen most prominent constellations in the sky, instantly readable to the untrained eye

## Planetary Use

In Aeron's tier-5 canon, `Drathalon` is the beast that mortals most often think of when they say "a giant of giants." Its descended kin walk multiple continents; the lineage is widespread because mass is hard to hide. Stories of fallen cities crushed by a striding shape, of mountain-vales formed by a single prone body, of titanwar between rival continents — all trace, in the deepest folk-memory, back to `Drathalon`.

## Boundaries

`Drathalon` must not be confused with the [Vatharen](vatharen.md) `Yirven` (Increase) or `Yirmor` (Decay), nor with planetary geological scale itself, nor with the geofauna and earth-elemental lineages that are descended from `Athravenel` (Wild-Heart). `Drathalon` is humanoid in form, no matter how vast. A walking mountain of stone is `Athravenel`'s; a vast humanoid that resembles a walking mountain is `Drathalon`'s.

## Relations

- [Vorothar](vorothar.md) is the class to which `Drathalon` belongs.
- [Athravenel](athravenel.md) (Wild-Heart) is a partner-edge; the two share scale but differ in form.

## Note On Form

`Drathalon` blends the new `Drath-` family (crushing-mass, scale past sense) with the `-alon` ending (walking-being, the moving-shape). The word names not the size alone but the union of size with motion — a mass that strides.
