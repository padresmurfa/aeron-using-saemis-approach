# Mirathven

- Rendered form: `Mirathven`
- Gloss: The Chimera-Mother; Cataclysmic Beast progenitor of all hybrid-form kin
- Tier: structural principle

## Meaning

`Mirathven` names the [Vorothar](vorothar.md) who births and braids the mixed-form lineages — the manticore, the griffin, the sphinx, the hippogriff, the peryton — every being whose body declares two natures as one.

## Scope

`Mirathven` carries:

- Cataclysmic hybridization power
- Braided-form sovereignty
- Progenitor-status for chimeras, manticores, griffins, sphinxes, hippogriffs, peryton, and every dual-natured legendary kin
- Riddle-aspect: the chimera-form that demands recognition before passage
- One of the fourteen most prominent constellations in the sky

## Planetary Use

In Aeron's tier-5 canon, `Mirathven` is the maker of mixings. Where two cataclysmic natures coexist in one body, `Mirathven` is the line through which that union descends. Mortal naturalists who study chimera-class beasts speak of "the Mother's hand" in the seam between part and part.

## Boundaries

`Mirathven` must not be confused with [Mirath](mirath.md), the existing structural principle of confined transformation pressure. Their roots share the `Mira-` family, but `Mirath` is fourth-order confinement intelligence and `Mirathven` is a Cataclysmic Beast. The relation is etymological, not ontological. `Mirathven` must also not be confused with [Khaethorom](khaethorom.md) (Form-Thief), who carries no fixed form at all; `Mirathven`'s offspring have stable hybrid bodies, while `Khaethorom`'s offspring have no body that holds.

## Relations

- [Vorothar](vorothar.md) is the class to which `Mirathven` belongs.
- [Mirath](mirath.md) shares the `Mira-` family but is a structural principle, not a beast.
- [Khaethorom](khaethorom.md) is the formless `Vorothar`; the two are often confused by untrained scholars but are wholly distinct.

## Note On Form

`Mirathven` blends the existing `Mira-` family (transformation-pressure, confined-change) with the new `-thven` ending (braided-form bearer). The word names a being whose own form is the canonical example of two-bodies-as-one, and whose offspring inherit the same braid.
