# Skaelmorath — The Wraith-Sovereign

`Skaelmorath`, the **Wraith-Sovereign**, is the [Vorothar](../../primal_language/structural_principles/vorothar.md) who rules from the other side of dying. Crowned in pale fire and grave-dust; the progenitor of every lich, dread-knight, banshee-queen, dread-spectre, and nazgul-class deathless ruler.

## Aspect and Domain

`Skaelmorath` is the cosmic principle of deathless sovereignty. The descended kin are beings who **passed through dying and continued to rule**. They are not undead in the sense of resurrected corpses (that is regional and various). They are beings whose authority survived the breath-edge intact — the will, the office, the crown, the oath all unbroken — and whose body is now whatever shape best supports continued sovereignty without breath.

## Visual Form

`Skaelmorath` is described in folk-memory as **a crowned figure in pale shroud**, the body humanoid in proportion but unbreathing, the face most often veiled (some traditions show the face skull-bare, others show it preserved-young, others show it ash-marked). The crown is canonical: an ancient diadem visibly older than any mortal sovereignty, fitted to a head that no longer needs warmth or blood. The hand bears a great ring of stewardship-metal. The voice, when heard, is dry as stone-rubbed-on-stone.

## Mana-Flavor

The `Skaelmorath` mana-flavor is **the cold throne**. It feels like authority that does not require the consent of breath. Mortals near `Skaelmorath`-kin experience the felt presence of unbreakable command: the urge to obey, to kneel, to recognize sovereignty even in unfamiliar form. Mages of this lineage are rare and singular: their oaths bind beyond death; their commands hold weight even when the speaker should logically have no authority. Necromancers of `Skaelmorath`'s mana are not corpse-raisers; they are oath-keepers across the breath-edge.

## Gaze-Resolution at Peak Attunement

A fully attuned viewer of the Wraith-Sovereign's constellation sees `Skaelmorath` positioned between themselves and the sky-pattern, in formal regalia, seated upon a throne that is itself ancient. `Skaelmorath` is **aware** of being attended. The reply, when it comes, takes the form of judgment — `Skaelmorath` does not converse in the way the other `Vorothar` do; the Sovereign's reply is a verdict on the gazer's standing. Mortals receiving the verdict have, in folk-memory, sometimes accepted vows from `Skaelmorath` that bound them across their own subsequent dying.

## Awareness and Interaction

`Skaelmorath` is **aware** of every wraith-kin descendant and of every mortal sovereign whose authority has crossed the breath-edge. Prayer to `Skaelmorath` is canonical for: oaths sworn to be kept past the swearer's death, monarchies seeking continuity through dynastic crisis, mages binding contracts that must outlast the contractors. The reply is solemn, formal, often constitutional in tone. `Skaelmorath` does not make spontaneous gifts; the Sovereign makes terms.

## Dwelling and Dormancy

`Skaelmorath` dwells dormant in a great frozen mausoleum-throne complex deep within the interior of [Kethrarvel](../places/kethrarvel.md), the Frostbound North, beneath ice and stone. Mortal architecture and statecraft know the site by various names; pilgrim-mortals visit it as a place where the breath-edge thins. The dormant body sits upon the throne in formal posture, and folk-memory holds that the dormancy is itself a continuous act of state — `Skaelmorath` is **ruling** while dormant, in slow time, by the mere fact of presence. The frostbound climate of Kethrarvel preserves the throne complex against decay.

## Stirring

`Skaelmorath` stirs:
- when a mortal sovereignty dishonors a sworn oath of state at a continental scale
- when the line of canonical kingship-by-oath is broken in a way that cannot be remedied by mortal rite
- when a wraith-kin descendant claims authority that crosses into the territory of breath-bearing sovereigns
- when the multi-millennial mythic cycle of the Sovereign's tenure renews

A stirred `Skaelmorath` is a continental judgment-event: the wraith-court rides, sovereignty contests are decided by the Sovereign's standing-of-judgment, and mortal kingdoms reform around the new verdicts. The stirring is rarely violent in the body-count sense; the stirring is constitutional. Defeating a stirred `Skaelmorath` is canonically held to be impossible by force; the Sovereign's verdicts are answered by counter-verdicts from pantheon-leaders, not by combat.

## Descended Kin

The wraith-kin lineage includes: liches (mage-class, oath-of-knowledge across death), dread-knights (warrior-class, oath-of-service across death), banshees (lament-class, often female-aspected, sovereign-by-grief), dread-spectres (lower-rank wraith-officers, often haunting), nazgul-class (multi-membered orders of wraith-knights bound to a single foundational oath), tomb-monarchs (regional sovereigns of dynastic crypt-cities).

Hybrid offspring: `Skaelmorath` × `Vorthalen` produces hellforge-knights (fire-shrouded wraiths); `Skaelmorath` × `Krevoraen` produces blood-wraiths (rare, the canonical "vampire-king" of folk-memory).

## Mythic Role

`Skaelmorath` in folk-memory is the cosmic underwriter of constitutional sovereignty. Mortal civilizations that build long-duration monarchies, durable empires, or oath-bound republics often locate their founding in some pact with the Sovereign. Cultures with strong rule-of-law traditions revere the Wraith-Sovereign as the guarantor that authority does not vanish with the death of any particular ruler.

## Canon Boundaries

- `Skaelmorath`'s dwelling continent and mausoleum-throne site are open canon.
- The exact identity (gender, dynastic origin, name behind the title) of `Skaelmorath` is canonically held to be **not known to mortals**, and this is preserved as canon: the Sovereign rules from beyond mortal naming.
- Specific named wraith-kin (named liches, named dread-knight orders) are downstream lesser-beast canon.
- The relationship between `Skaelmorath`'s authority and the personal-deity gods of any pantheon is continent-local mythic tradition.
